# Alex Hormozi — Traffic Blocks & Designing the Day

Research notes for the Flock / Illumination Project operating system. Two ideas
sit under this: **how Hormozi generates "traffic" (attention → leads)** and
**how he designs a day so that traffic work actually gets made.** The daily
8am / 1pm / 8pm status cadence is built on top of both.

---

## Part 1 — "Traffic" = The Core Four (from *$100M Leads*)

Hormozi's claim: there are only **four ways** to let people know about what you
do. Everything else is a flavor of one of these. He calls them the **Core Four**.

| # | Method | What it is | Flock / IP version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Warm outreach** | DM/text people who already know you, one-to-one | Text the phone list + reply to every Skool/IG DM about the Creator Games |
| 2 | **Free content (organic)** | Post publicly, one-to-many, no ask | YouTube long-form (Tue/Fri), Shorts/Reels, 3 stories/day, daily Skool post |
| 3 | **Cold outreach** | Reach strangers one-to-one (email/DM lists) | Follow-Up email sequence to the cold/warm list (the 🏆 CG Follow-Up 1–7 tasks) |
| 4 | **Paid ads** | Pay to reach strangers one-to-many | GHL-scheduled paid promo when there's budget behind a launch |

**Sequencing Hormozi recommends** (do them in this order as you scale):
1. **Warm outreach first** — it gets your first customers fastest (he says you can
   get your first ~5 just from your existing contacts).
2. **Free content** to build authority and become the go-to in the niche.
3. **Cold outreach + paid ads** once there's a proven offer to scale.

> The Flock funnel is currently running #1 + #2 + #3 simultaneously through the
> Creator Games launch (warm texts, daily organic Skool/IG, cold follow-up email
> series). Paid (#4) is the lever left to pull.

**A "traffic block"** = a protected block of the day whose *only* job is Core-Four
work — making and shipping the content/outreach that brings in attention. It is
NOT admin, editing logistics, or email. It is the actual promoting.

## Part 2 — Designing the Day

### The 4/4/4 split (how Hormozi divides business work)
Every hour of business work falls into one of three buckets. He aims for **~4
hours each** on a full day:

- **Building** — product, systems, infrastructure (courses, Skool build-out, templates)
- **Promoting** — marketing, sales, content, lead-gen → *this is your traffic block*
- **Delivering** — fulfillment, ops, customer support (demos, member onboarding, calls)

Solo operators start smaller: a **2/2/2** split, then grow. Schedule your
**weakest** bucket first (for most creators that's Promoting — so protect the
traffic block before anything else). An imperfect 40/35/25 beats the typical
80/15/5 that most people drift into.

### Maker vs. Manager time
- **Makers** (writing songs, producing, scripting, editing, building courses) need
  **large sacred blocks — 4–6 hrs, only 1–3 per day.** You cannot make anything
  great in 30-minute slivers.
- **Managers** (meetings, calls, approvals, DMs) run on fragmented time.
- **Never mix them.** A single 30-min meeting dropped into a maker block doesn't
  cost 30 minutes — *it costs the whole block*, because context-switching back into
  deep focus takes 20–30 minutes of recovery each time.

### Protect peak hours — schedule meetings *backwards*
Hormozi's mornings and early afternoons are his peak. So his team **books
meetings from the END of the day backwards**: the first meeting anyone can grab
next week is 5:00pm, then 4:30, then 4:00… working earlier only as the late slots
fill. Result: **mornings and early afternoons stay 100% free for maker/traffic
work**, and he stays in focus mode from ~5am until early afternoon.

### Fortress rules (how the block stays protected)
- Deep work happens in a **specific place with specific rules**; meetings happen
  elsewhere, batched.
- **Phone on Do-Not-Disturb.** Team trained not to interrupt unless it's on fire.
- **Batch like with like** — e.g. all brainstorm/record on one day, all edits on
  another (his media crew batches brainstorms into Thursdays).
- **Protect the evening** (after ~7pm) the same way the morning is protected.

## Part 3 — How this maps to your day (8am / 1pm / 8pm)

The status cadence is designed around Hormozi's structure:

| Time | Hormozi principle | What the ping does |
|---|---|---|
| **8:00 AM** | Peak maker hours start — protect them. Name the **Big 3** before the day fragments. | Status update + **Big 3 focus** (the 3 highest-leverage things, tagged Build / Promote / Deliver) |
| **1:00 PM** | Midday checkpoint before the afternoon "traffic block." | Progress check + reset the afternoon: what still has to ship today |
| **8:00 PM** | Evening is protected maker time; batch a focused sprint + one stretch project. | **Evening sprint** (one concrete block) + a **late-night project** to reach for only if energy allows |

**Big 3 rule:** three things, no more. At least one is always a **Promote**
(traffic) item — because for a creator that's the weakest-scheduled bucket and the
one that compounds. If the Creator Games launch has a send due that day, that send
is almost always Big-3 #1.
